# M4.2 locked natural-shift confirmation protocol

**Freeze point:** 2026-10-21, before downloading or opening UCI shopper outcome labels. Commit this file before access. All earlier ACS, hospital, bank and 2025 College final outcomes are viewed and are excluded from confirmatory method selection.

## Source evidence and fixed candidates

Nested source-domain evaluation used 7 household-disjoint ACS states, 4 patient-disjoint hospital admission codes, and 2 forward-only bank blocks. The metadata router improved Brier over a validation-selected fixed expert on 6/13 outer domains, with family mean Brier ACS 0.141437 vs 0.141614, hospital 0.189170 vs 0.189329, bank 0.057372 vs 0.056951. A simpler two-feature ridge router was inconsistent. Source-validation-weighted five-expert ensemble mean Brier was ACS 0.141332, hospital 0.188566, bank 0.056665. These small ensemble gains cost five fitted experts. Existing prior final-domain router failures remain negative audit evidence.

Evaluate exactly six probability policies: source-validation-selected fixed expert, uniform five-expert mean, inverse-source-Brier weighted ensemble, source-only sigmoid-calibrated fixed expert, original 12-metadata Ridge(alpha=100) five-risk router trained on the three existing families' domain-held-out source predictions, and a simpler two-feature-per-expert Ridge(alpha=100) risk router trained on those same source predictions. The simplified router uses each expert probability and five-expert standard deviation, with equal total training weight per family and per source environment. No hyperparameter search, new expert, shift correction, or target-label adaptation is permitted. Five fixed expert configurations are those in `make_experts(..., seed=264738, advanced=True)`. The fixed expert alone is the default production candidate because its extra-expert cost is zero; the five-expert methods are resource-matched to each other.

## Fresh family, data and outcome

[UCI Online Shoppers Purchasing Intention, ID 468](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/468/online+shoppers+purchasing+intention+dataset) is a public 12,330-session, one-year dataset under CC BY 4.0. Target is `Revenue == True` (completed purchase), evaluated at a hypothetical pre-checkout scoring point after observed page browsing. **Exclude** `PageValues` (potential outcome leakage), `BounceRates` and `ExitRates` (uncertain same-session timing), `Month` (domain key), and `Revenue`. Use exactly these 13 predictors: Administrative, Administrative_Duration, Informational, Informational_Duration, ProductRelated, ProductRelated_Duration, SpecialDay, OperatingSystems, Browser, Region, TrafficType, VisitorType, Weekend. Treat OperatingSystems, Browser, Region, TrafficType and VisitorType as categorical, and Weekend as binary numeric. Imputation, scaling and encoding fit on source fit rows only. The UCI description says sessions are from different users, but no entity IDs are supplied to independently verify this. No exact within-month dates are supplied, so month-level ordering is all that can be enforced; this is a derived seasonal benchmark, not a causal prospective deployment study.

The month split is fixed: fit = Feb, Mar, May, June, Jul, Aug; selection = Sep; probability calibration = first half of Oct rows in supplied order; threshold calibration = second half of Oct rows; untouched confirmation environments = Nov and Dec, evaluated separately and combined only descriptively. Source months and target months must be present. If any required partition has fewer than 100 rows or only one label class, **stop confirmation** rather than revise the split. CSV row order within October is not assumed to be exact time; it is only a deterministic disjoint calibration partition. No target row or label enters fitting, expert choice, calibration, router training, or source thresholds.

## Objectives, selective policy and decision rule

Primary metric is Brier; log loss is co-primary safety check. Also report AUROC, AUPRC, 10-bin ECE, prediction prevalence, ID-to-OOD differences, fit/inference seconds and peak working set. For each policy use confidence and five-expert disagreement as rejection scores. Source threshold-calibration rows fix the 80th percentile uncertainty cutoff. Report realized target acceptance and accepted error; below 80% target acceptance fails the deployed selective objective. A separate **transductive batch** policy accepts the 80% lowest-uncertainty *unlabeled target* scores, without labels, and is explicitly not an online decision rule. Risk–coverage curves are descriptive and never used to choose methods or thresholds.

Decision A (ADAPTIVE ROUTER SUPPORTED) requires the router to beat both fixed and weighted ensemble in mean Brier in **every** nested source family, and on both Nov and Dec by at least 0.002 absolute Brier with no worse log loss. The first source condition is already unmet, so the new confirmation cannot alone reverse that verdict. Decision B (SELECTIVE RELIABILITY SUPPORTED) requires a simpler fixed or ensemble confidence/disagreement policy to meet actual >=80% acceptance and lower accepted error than the fixed-confidence policy on both months, without worse Brier by more than 0.002. Otherwise decision C (NO ADAPTIVE ADVANTAGE DEMONSTRATED). A and B are research claims limited to these environments; no row-IID significance test. The two target months are related, so no independent-family confidence interval or broad shift guarantee is claimed. If C, retain the source-selected fixed expert as the production baseline and routers as research-only prototypes; weighted ensemble remains an optional higher-cost comparison. Do not revise criteria after target inspection.
