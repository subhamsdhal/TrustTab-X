# Dated research and licence review — 2026-10-20

This is a short source check for experimental design, not a novelty or performance claim.

1. **Foundation models.** Hollmann et al., [*Accurate predictions on small data with a tabular foundation model*](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08328-6), Nature (2025), motivates a TabPFN research comparison but reports a different experimental setup and compute budget. It does not establish TrustTab-X performance. The [current TabPFN repository](https://github.com/PriorLabs/TabPFN) identifies TabPFN-3.5 as its default. Its **code** is Apache-2.0; its **3.5 model weights** have a separate non-commercial, non-production [licence](https://huggingface.co/Prior-Labs/tabpfn_3_5/blob/main/LICENSE). Checkpoint access may require authentication/licence acceptance. No TabPFN checkpoint was downloaded or benchmarked here. A commercial service must exclude those weights absent a commercial licence.
2. **Strong tabular baselines.** Chen and Guestrin, [*XGBoost: A Scalable Tree Boosting System*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.02754), KDD (2016), and Prokhorenkova et al., [*CatBoost: unbiased boosting with categorical features*](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2018/hash/14491b756b3a51daac41c24863285549-Abstract.html), NeurIPS (2018), justify competitive tree baselines. Grinsztajn et al., [*Why do tree-based models still outperform deep learning on typical tabular data?*](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/hash/0378c7692da36807bdec87ab043cdadc-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks.html), NeurIPS (2022), cautions against weak baseline comparisons. The current fixed-configuration pilot is not a resource-matched tuning study.
3. **Conformal and selective prediction.** Angelopoulos and Bates, [*A Gentle Introduction to Conformal Prediction and Distribution-Free Uncertainty Quantification*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07511), describes the exchangeability-based marginal guarantee and its assumptions. Gibbs and Candès, [*Adaptive Conformal Inference Under Distribution Shift*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.00170), addresses an online setting with a different guarantee; its results cannot be transferred to this fixed split-conformal implementation. Geifman and El-Yaniv, [*Selective Classification for Deep Neural Networks*](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2017/file/4a8423d5e91fda00bb7e46540e2b0cf1-Paper.pdf), motivates risk–coverage reporting. The current confidence-ranked AURC is descriptive and carries no selective-risk guarantee.
4. **Shift monitoring.** Rabanser, Günnemann and Lipton, [*Failing Loudly: An Empirical Study of Methods for Detecting Dataset Shift*](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2019/hash/846c260d715e5b854ffad5f70a516c88-Abstract.html), NeurIPS (2019), studies detection methods and limitations. An alarm does not identify causal mechanisms or certify prediction safety. The M3 feature-corruption and prior-resampling tests are controlled stressors, not observed natural domain shift.
5. **Probability calibration.** Guo et al., [*On Calibration of Modern Neural Networks*](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/guo17a.html), ICML (2017), motivates separate calibration diagnostics. The current equal-width 10-bin ECE is a descriptive estimate, not proof of individual probability accuracy.

The [OpenML benchmarking suite documentation](https://openml.github.io/docs/benchmark/) and [task split API](https://docs.openml.org/reference/tasks/task/) were used for the M1 task and fold protocol. Source details and literal licence metadata for the chosen datasets are in `docs/OPENML_TASKS.md`.
